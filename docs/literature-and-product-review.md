# Literature and product review

**Katherine Lin · The Listening Room**

## Search catalogue

I searched academic databases using combinations of older adults, technology use, digital literacy, music listening, wellbeing, music streaming, usability, voice interfaces and self-efficacy. The catalogue below consolidates duplicated entries from the working spreadsheet. Music therapy and rehabilitation literature provide context on the meanings of music. The design uses the listening product as an everyday tool.

| Source | Research lens | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| [Tsai, Shillair and Cotten, *Social Support and Playing Around*](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2418100) | How older adults acquire digital literacy through exploration and social support. | Make exploration reversible and support independent retry. |
| [Elliot et al., *Predictors of Older Adults’ Technology Use*](https://doi.org/10.1093/geronb/gbt109) | Technology use, individual differences and wellbeing. | Treat digital comfort as variable. Ask about prior habits and support. |
| [Schak et al., *Smart Home for the Elderly*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-34917-1_5) | Comparison of interaction techniques. | Offer screen, voice and tactile controls where each fits. |
| [Chang and Lee, *Applying PBL Model to Technological Learning Strategies for Older Adults*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-34917-1_30) | Learning technology through contextual tasks. | Let people practise remote actions with immediate feedback. |
| Zhao, *Value Co-creation Perspectives on Digital Literacy Training for Older Adults* | Digital literacy support and the place of the learner. | Test guidance with listeners, including whether they feel able to repeat a task. |
| [Zhao et al., *Music Therapy for Older Adults With Depression*](https://doi.org/10.1002/gps.4494) | Evidence from music therapy settings. | Ask about the personal meaning of listening and avoid inferring a clinical outcome. |
| [Mangiacotti et al., *The Impact of Community Music Therapy Intervention in Older Adults’ Care Home Communities*](https://doi.org/10.1386/ijcm_00096_1) | Music in social and care settings. | Explore ways for shared music to support ongoing contact. |
| [Ansah et al., *Prototype Features for a Sleep-Enhancing Music App in Older Adults Living With Dementia*](https://doi.org/10.1093/geroni/igaf122.3188) | Accessible music interface features for a specific population. | Evaluate scalable text, labels and clear layouts with the intended audience. |
| [Quinn et al., *Assessing the Feasibility and Acceptability of Smart Speakers in Behavioral Intervention Research With Older Adults*](https://doi.org/10.2196/54800) | Feasibility and acceptability of voice interfaces. | Make voice an optional input and test recovery across accents and languages. |
| [Särkämö et al., *Music, Brain, and Rehabilitation*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00103) | Background on music and rehabilitation research. | Use memory and routine as interview topics. |
| [Mangiacotti et al., *Effects of Music Therapy in Elderly Care*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jalz.2019.06.3500) | Exploratory evidence across cognitive, mood and social domains. | Treat emotional or social benefits as questions for future study. |
| [Melhuish, Beuzeboc and Guzmán, *Developing Relationships Between Care Staff and People With Dementia Through Music Therapy and Dance Movement Therapy*](https://doi.org/10.1177/1471301215588030) | Relationship building through shared activity. | Support shared playlists with a familiar, low-effort path. |
| [Powell, *The Voice of Experience*](https://doi.org/10.1177/13594575060200020) | Music therapy across community settings. | Consider listening context when interpreting participant reports. |

## Product review

The desk review compared how five services handled a known-song search, a return to saved music and access through a familiar device. Observations describe the designs reviewed during the project.

| Service | Pattern examined | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Spotify | Saved music, recommendations and returning to a prior session. | Make liked songs and recent playback explicit destinations. |
| Apple Music | Familiar library conventions, album browsing and navigation depth. | Use a recognisable library model with clear names and fewer decisions per screen. |
| YouTube Music | Searching for remembered titles and the familiarity of video-based listening. | Preserve typed search and recent queries as dependable entry points. |
| Amazon Music | Voice ecosystem and choice of device or speaker. | Give voice and output separate, visible controls with clear feedback. |
| KuGou Music | Music catalogue and listening conventions for Chinese-language music. | Include heritage music in evaluation and account for title variants and code-switching. |

The opportunity I selected was quick and confident return to personally meaningful music. I prioritised stable navigation and repairable search before exploration or recommendation.
